### B1 (blocker): the rail forces the whole page to 1200px wide at desktop widths 1024 to about 1200
- **What happens:** while the rail is active, `main`'s grid track resolves to `1200px` (`getComputedStyle(main).gridTemplateColumns === '1200px'`), whatever the viewport width.
- **Cause:** the rail's list is `width: max-content` (about 8,856px). Its min-content contribution goes up through `#work > .container` (capped at `--container` = 1200) into `main`'s auto grid track. `overflow: clip` on `.work-rail` does not remove that min-content contribution.
- **1024×900:** `scrollWidth` is 1320 against `innerWidth` 1024. The hero, stats and every section are laid out 1200px wide and clipped on the right by `body { overflow-x: clip }`. Screenshot: `.company/qa/shots/T-025-BUG-1024-hero.png`.
- **1100×800:** `scrollWidth` is 1282 against `innerWidth` 1100.
- **At 1440 and 1920** the same effect is hidden, because the container is already 1200. It causes B1b instead.
- **B1b, the pinned rail is not full-bleed:** when ScrollTrigger pins the rail, it writes `width: 1200px; max-width: 1200px` inline. With `box-sizing: border-box` and 120px inline padding, that leaves a 960px window, clipped at x = 1200 on a 1440 viewport.
  - Cards are hard-cut at 1200, and the right 240px is empty: `shots/T-025-rail-50-1440x900-dark.png`, `T-025-rail-hover-opensource-1440.png`, and the video.
  - The progress bar spans 120 to 1080 instead of 120 to 1320.
  - The tween's `x` (`-dist()` = -7,896) is no longer the pin length (`end - start` = 7,656), so the 1:1 mapping is off by 240px.
  - In keyboard focus, the third card ends at 1228 > 1200 (28px clipped), because `focus()` checks against `innerWidth` rather than the rail's visible box.
- **Fix direction (tech lead to choose):**
  - Stop the rail's intrinsic width from leaking out, e.g. `contain: inline-size` (or `width: 100vw` plus `min-width: 0`) on `.rail-on .work-rail`, and/or `minmax(0, 1fr)` on `main`'s track (but `main` is outside T-025's files).
  - Make the pinned box full-bleed. One option is to pin an outer wrapper that has no inline padding and put the padding on an inner track. Another is `pinSpacing`/`pinReparent` options, or forcing the width with an `!important` rule.
  - Re-check: rail rect = [0, innerWidth] while pinned, `-x_end == end - start`, and `scrollWidth == innerWidth` at 1024/1100/1280/1440/1920.

### B3 (blocker): the rail randomly never engages, because the fit check races 30-filter's initial `min-height`
- **What happens:** 30-filter's first `render()` sets `#project-list.style.minHeight` to the grid card height (about 705–732px) and clears it after 200ms.
- **Why the rail falls back:** when the `B.ready` flush runs inside that window (t ≈ 240–390ms after navigation, as on fast or warm loads), `list.offsetHeight` reads 705/732/758 instead of 607. That exceeds the 728 limit, so the rail falls back to the grid. Because `mm` only re-runs on a media change, the fallback is permanent for that page view.
- **Evidence:**
  - I instrumented `offsetHeight` at the moment of the check: `"st":"min-height: 731.719px;"` in every failing load, and `""` in every passing load.
  - All cards' natural content heights were identical between passing and failing loads.
  - Resizing 1440→900→1440 rebuilds the rail correctly.
- **Frequency:**
  - 4 of 10, 2 of 6 and 3 of 8 loads at 1440×900/1920×1080 in quiet runs.
  - 12 of 12 under machine load (load average about 20).
  - 1280×800 and 1920×1080-dark fell back in the matrix run.
- **Fix direction:** make the check deterministic. Some options:
  - Measure without the inline `min-height` (read and restore it, or measure the tallest card's natural height).
  - Defer the check until after 30-filter releases the height.
  - Re-evaluate eligibility on `B.refresh`.
  - Add a vm/browser test that sets `#project-list.style.minHeight = '732px'` before the ready flush.

### B2 (major): the first card is not scrolled into view when focus enters the rail
- **What happens:** with Tab from the last filter chip into the rail, the first card's link ends up at left = -80 (card -80..340, clipped by the rail edge, so the focused link is partly off-screen). This violates WCAG 2.4.7.
- **Cause:** `focusin` decides "already visible" synchronously. The browser's native focus scroll and the 0.6s scrub then move the list afterwards, so the early return was wrong.
- **Fix direction:** always compute the target `y` for the focused card (it is cheap). Alternatively, check visibility against the tween's target position, not the current rect.
- **Result:** the remaining 27 focus stops, through 20 cards, were all fully visible.

## T-027: code review
- `await a.ready.catch(()=>{finish()})` together with `if(done)return` is correct.
- The whole entrance setup is in `try { … } catch { finish() }`, and `finish()` uses `try { tl?.progress(1) } finally { revert() }`, so the name is restored even if GSAP throws.
- A hard watchdog `setTimeout(finish, max(0, 3000 - performance.now()))` is added and cleared in `revert()`.
- The vm test covers 4 paths: cancelled, pending, error and stalled.
- Browser simulation (1440, intro on), time until the name is fully visible:
  - normal: 2,108ms;
  - a real `cancel()` of the pending `intro-out`: 1,736ms;
  - a rejected `ready`: 319ms;
  - a `ready` that never settles: 3,028ms;
  - a GSAP timeline that never plays: 3,019ms.
- The last two are the watchdog firing at 3,000ms plus my 25ms polling granularity.
- **Nit (optional):** the watchdog fires exactly at the 3s budget with no margin, so on a busy main thread the visible frame lands just after 3s. Consider 2,800ms if the budget is meant strictly.
